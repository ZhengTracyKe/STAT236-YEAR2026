Cross-border venture capital kept globalising. Its bridge to China broke, twice.

We model the global VC network as a degree-corrected mixed-membership network and let a change-point detector find when its structure moved. China-related features break in 2019. Global features break a year later. The US–China pair itself decouples from 2022, hardest in AI and deep tech.

Where cross-border VC went, year by year

China-related ties peaked at 16.5% of all cross-border ties in 2018, then fell to 9.5% in 2019 and 3.8% by 2024. Over the same years the cross-border share of all VC ties kept rising, from 19% in 2010 to 31% in 2024.

2 · Country structure

The VC triangle: countries as mixtures of three blocs

Mixed-SCORE on the 51-country network (K = 3) puts every country inside a triangle. Its corners are the Atlantic core, Asia-Pacific and an emerging periphery, and a country's position is its membership mix. Move the window to watch positions drift; click a country to trace it.

Selected country

The US sits near the centre (0.38 / 0.34 / 0.28): it is the hub that links all three blocs. Positions are sticky. Singapore drifts toward the Atlantic core, and China drifts slightly away from Asia-Pacific. The country-level change is mostly in how much China invests across borders, not in where it sits.

3 · Investor communities

China's co-investment community became an island

Nodes are the 1,600 most-connected investors. They are linked when they co-invested in at least two rounds within the window, and laid out by force. Hover a node to see the investor; search to find one.

In 2015–17 the China community (86% China-HQ) hung off the US core, and 84% of its ties stayed internal. By 2022–24 it is 93% China-HQ and 92% of its ties are internal. Its average estimated connectivity to the other communities halved, from 0.38 to 0.18.

4 · Letting the data choose the date

China-related features break in 2019. Global features break in 2020.

We computed network features every quarter on trailing 12-month windows and ran multivariate binary segmentation, choosing the number of breaks by BIC. Pick a series to inspect.

break, China-related features (2014 Q2, 2019 Q2)break, global features without China (2014 Q2, 2020 Q3)

The China break comes five quarters before the global one. The window ending in 2019 Q2 spans the tariff rounds, FIRRMA and the CFIUS pilot, and China's tightening of domestic fund-raising. The global break lines up with COVID-era remote deal-making and the 2021 boom. In 82% of bootstrap replications the China break lands in 2019 Q2.

5 · Attribution

First China withdrew, then the pair split

The DCMM model separates a node's activity (θ) from its pair-specific ties. A gravity event study removes every country's own boom and bust, so what remains is the US–China pair itself.

Degree parameter θ̂ from the Poisson DCMM, as a share of the total. China's share halves in 2019.

PPML event study, log points vs. 2017, with pair and country×year fixed effects. With so few treated pairs, the bars are illustrative; inference uses permutation ranks.

Observed ÷ predicted ties under a DCMM fitted on 2014–17 (pre-period = 1). The band shows the 10th–90th percentiles across 78 large country pairs.

The 2019 break is mostly an activity shock to the China node: China's θ̂ share fell from 5.4% to 3.0% in one year. The pair-specific decline arrives from 2022. US–China ties fall 39% in 2022 and 63% by 2024 relative to 2017, ranking 2nd and 5th of 78 large pairs. That path looks like Russia–West after the 2022 sanctions.

6 · Industries

The split is deepest in AI, deep tech and industrial sectors

A triple difference compares each industry's US–China ties with the same industry in all other country pairs.

2019–212022–24

AI & deep tech (−0.81 log points in 2022–24) and industrial & energy (−1.22) show the largest and most persistent drops. Consumer ties fell in 2019–21 and then stabilised. Healthcare barely moved until 2022. The pattern tracks the policies' focus on critical and dual-use technology.

7 · The bridges

The investors who connected both markets stepped back

A bridge investor has at least 20% of its co-investors headquartered in China and at least 20% in the US. Pick firms to compare their share of China-based co-investors over time.

Bridges made up 2–2.6% of the network in windows centred on 2014–2019, then fell to 0.5%. Crossover investors such as Tencent, Temasek, Coatue, Tiger Global and SoftBank's Vision Fund all cut their China co-investor share after 2019. Dedicated China funds such as IDG, Qiming, HongShan and MPC stayed inside the China community.

Data

PitchBook (April 2026 extract): 1.08M participations in 372,731 completed seed, early-stage and later-stage VC rounds, 2010–2024. Individual angels are excluded. Countries are the investor's and the startup's headquarters, with mainland China separate from Hong Kong.

Network methods

DCMM model; SCORE and SCORE+ normalisation; Mixed-SCORE with sketched vertex hunting (k-means sketch plus successive projection); K chosen by eigen-ratio; block matrix P̂ estimated from Π̂ᵀAΠ̂. Country network built with the WTO trade-network recipe from Lecture 10.

Change and attribution

Multivariate binary segmentation (ℓ₂ cost, BIC with effective n/4) with a block bootstrap; PPML structural gravity with permutation ranks; Poisson DCMM link prediction; industry triple differences. All results are preliminary and observational.
